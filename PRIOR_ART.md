# Literature comparison — 26 November 2026 (JST)

## Direct comparison with Sathiyabama and Monikandan

S. Sathiyabama and S. Monikandan, *On the reconstruction number of generalized join of graphs*, Discrete Mathematics 349 (2026), 114881. DOI: 10.1016/j.disc.2025.114881. The full article (11 pages) was inspected. Section 5, pp. 7–8, Theorem 9 and Corollary 10 are directly relevant.

Their construction includes our entire family, via base graph K2, k=b, n=s=c-b. Each base vertex is replaced by a clique of size b+1 with b matched endpoints, then the single unmatched vertex is replaced by a clique of size s. The resulting graph is F(c,c,b). Theorem 9 provides rn >= min(b+1,s+2), strictly when b>=s+1. In conjunction with McMullen and Radziszowski's 2007 bound rn>=4, the compared lower bounds are 4 for b=2,s>=2; b+1 for 3<=b<=s; s+3 for b>=s+1,s>=2; and 4 for s=1. Our corresponding exact values are 4, b+2, s+3, and max(5,c). Thus some prior lower bounds already have the correct numerical value. We supply general proofs, including the matching upper bounds, and the complete blocker criterion; do not claim the original family or existing lower-bound witnesses as new. Section 6 studies restricted class reconstruction numbers, not the unrestricted quantities here. No exact formula or equivalent blocker theorem was located in the supplied full text.

McMullen and Radziszowski (2007), §2.2.4, Theorem 5 and Table 2, provide the earlier lower bound and six computed ally values, and explicitly leave the general exact calculation open. Their construction includes the mixed unbalanced competitor F(c+1,c-1,b). Its mixed competitor shares $b+(s+1)=c+1$ cards of the stated types with the target, yielding the adversary lower bound $c+2$ directly. Section 3 also uses the one-vertex extension enumeration framework. The 2010 survey by Asciak et al. was also checked. The 2026 article's introduction states that Brewster et al. (2012) exclude H_n^k; the 2012 full text has not been inspected. Public arXiv index and GitHub searches did not identify an identical general result, but cannot establish absolute priority.

## The 2009 computational precedent

D. Rivshin and S. P. Radziszowski, *The vertex and edge graph reconstruction numbers of small graphs*, Australasian Journal of Combinatorics 45 (2009), 175–188, [§2](https://www.cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/paper59.pdf#page=4) and [Table 5](https://www.cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/paper59.pdf#page=6). The paper identifies the same family under the notation $K_c\leftrightarrow^b K_c$, gives both exact numbers in all 21 cases for $3\leq c\leq 8$ and $2\leq b<c$, and explicitly calls out $c+2$ as the observed adversary pattern. The six cases from 2007 are contained in those 21 cases. The seven $c=9$ cases in this repository extend that table. The general upper bound and full blocker criterion are not in the cited §2 or Table 5; these, and the general exact ally formula, are the defensible contributions, subject to literature limits. The case $(c,b)=(3,2)$ is a replication for both numbers, not an original numerical result.
